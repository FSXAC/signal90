Signal/90

Emissive type on a dead-black tube. Flat screens, boxed labels, stretched Helvetica, alarms that strobe on the anime frame. Studied from a 305-second, 608-cut supercut of late-90s anime command-center graphics. Everything below is original.

What the UI does

Nothing eases. Every change lands on the anime frame grid (12 drawings/s): blinks, cell flips, bar steps, counters. Critical states strobe at one frame on, one frame off.

What the type does

One family, Helvetica Bold, stretched horizontally: squeezed tall to shout, a little narrow for labels, pulled wide for blunt warnings. Kanji stamps in a heavy Mincho. Terminal dumps in mono.

What the screen does

A flat black field, axis-aligned, filling the frame like a monitor. One or two emissive hues per shot. Red means fault, mint means nominal, amber means data.

02

Colour

色彩

Colours are emissive: they light the black, they don't sit on white. Allow at most three signal hues in one shot.

03

Typography

書体

One family, Helvetica Bold (Arimo as the web fallback), stretched with transform: scaleX() instead of picking condensed or extended cuts. That is how the source was made: phototype distorted on a stat camera. The .hx helper scales the glyphs and gives the lost width back with negative margins, so layout stays honest.

Motion

動作

Everything runs on the anime frame: --af: 83.333ms (24 fps drawn on twos). Animations use steps() and move in big jumps, so a state change is done within 2–6 anime frames. There is no camera: no tilt, no push, no parallax.

These are tube and signal artefacts, not camera effects. Toggle each one on the reel.

Layout rules

Flat screen. Everything is axis-aligned at 0°. The frame is the monitor bezel. No perspective, rotation or camera moves.
Bands. Stack full-width horizontal bands: hazard / kanji / display word / kanji / hazard.
Bleed. Display words may run off the left or right edge; the crop is the point.
Corner tags. Put object, live and magnification tags in the corners, inside a 2.5 cqw safe margin.
Crosshair field. A regular lattice of + marks with drifting × markers on targets.
Rulers. Signed tick scales (−5 … +5) along the edges.
Ornament over containment. Boxes are drawn because they look engineered, not to group content. Use cut-corner double frames, dashed bays, corner brackets, label tabs sitting on rails, and diamond cells.
Wire it up. Join related boxes with orthogonal connectors and dot terminals. Tie numbers to bars with leader lines.
Chain the data. Butt labelled cells against each other (key over value) instead of spacing them out.
Walls of text. Repeat one intimidating word until it becomes texture, or fill the field with dense dump text (hex, logs, binary) behind the hero. Edge columns of binary make good side rails.
Asymmetric, never modal. Heroes sit off-centre or bleed off an edge. Frames get one cut corner, an open side or a tab, not four matching corners. No centred dialog boxes; verdicts are full-width bands.
Overlap on purpose. Digits and hero words may run out past an open frame side or across a band. Never just past a closed, symmetric box: that reads as a bug.
Oblique is rare. Only a 7-segment hero readout may slant (.obl-seg, seg(…,-8)), at most one per screen. Titles, warnings and labels stay upright.
One idea per shot. Ornament and walls are the background. One hero word or one hero number sits on top.

07

Sound

音響

Documented from the second reference study, not implemented. Sound is its own layer: its texture tends to change at picture cuts (about 30% more than at random points), but it can hold steady across stutter cuts. Everything is pure tones: no music, no samples.

Bed

Sub-bass rumble, 30–120 Hz, under every screen. Louder on heavy screens.

Pulses

Very tonal low hits on an augmented triad: 194 · 248 · 312 · 388 Hz (≈ G3 B3 D♯4 G4), plus 151 and 345 Hz. Pulse rate 3.5–8 Hz. This is where the unease comes from.

Carrier

Steady sines around 1.3 kHz and 2.5 kHz (a harmonic pair) under data screens.

Beep train

A 1314 Hz beep every 90–130 ms during sweeps; a 6.5 kHz dash train during stutters.

Chatter

Random-pitch blips, 600–2400 Hz, every ~90 ms: the sound of the anime-frame grid.

Alarm · static · sequencer

Alarm pulses at ≈11–12 Hz, matching the visual strobe. Broadband static bursts as transition hits. A short sine-note sequencer (250–700 Hz) over a bass pulse for "processing" outros.

Machine-readable version: signal90/guide.json → sound.
